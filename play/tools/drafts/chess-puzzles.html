Practical Tips to Improve at Chess Puzzles

  • Look for checks first: Any move that puts the opponent's king in check is a strong candidate. It forces them to respond immediately, which can lead to winning material or checkmate.
  • Check for captures: If you can take a piece without losing anything, that's a good move. Captures often lead to immediate gains and can simplify the position.
  • Identify threats: Are there any pieces that can be attacked or taken if you don't act quickly? A good puzzle often has one key move that neutralizes a threat or creates a new one.
  • Use the hint button wisely: If you're stuck, the hint button can guide you toward the right direction without giving away the full solution. This helps you learn by doing.
  • Review the solution: Even if you solve a puzzle on your own, always read the explanation. It helps you understand the tactical theme and how to recognize similar patterns in the future.

Why This Skill Helps in Real Life

Solving chess puzzles trains your brain to think critically and make decisions under pressure. This skill is valuable in real-life situations such as exams, interviews, and problem-solving at work. Just like in chess, many challenges in life require you to identify the best move quickly and accurately.

It also helps in coding interviews and technical problem-solving, where recognizing patterns and thinking ahead are essential. The ability to break down complex problems into smaller, manageable steps is a skill you'll use every day, whether you're debugging code or planning a project.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to be a strong chess player?

No. You need to know how the pieces move, but nothing more. If you're new to chess, start with the Easy level: many of those puzzles are short checkmates or simple captures.

How do I know if my answer is correct?

After you make a move, the game will tell you if it's correct. If it's not, you can use the hint button or look at the solution to learn the right approach.

Can I track my progress?

Yes! The page shows how many puzzles you've solved and your current perfect streak. This helps you stay motivated and track your improvement over time.

Are the puzzles from real games?

Yes! All puzzles are taken from the Lichess puzzle database, which is released free into the public domain (CC0). Each puzzle comes from a real game, so you're practicing tactics that actually happen in chess matches.

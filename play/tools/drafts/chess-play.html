Practical Tips for Playing Better Chess

  • Control the center: Try to occupy the center squares (e4, d4, e5, d5) early in the game. This gives your pieces more space and better mobility.
  • Develop your pieces: Get your knights and bishops out early. Don’t waste time moving the same piece multiple times unless needed.
  • Protect your king: Castle early to keep your king safe and connect your rooks. A king that’s left in the center is vulnerable to attacks.
  • Look for tactics: Watch for opportunities like pins, forks, and captures. At the lower levels the computer makes mistakes on purpose, so look for pieces it leaves undefended.
  • Use the take-back feature: If you make a mistake, use the take-back button to try a different move. It’s a great way to learn and improve.

Why Chess Skills Help in Real Life

Playing chess helps you think strategically, plan ahead, and make better decisions under pressure. These are useful skills for exams, coding interviews, and even daily problem-solving at work or in life.

When you play against the computer, you're not just learning chess—you're training your brain to be more focused, patient, and analytical. These are skills that can help you in school, interviews, and many other areas of life.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I play as Black or White?

Yes, you can choose to play as White or Black.

What happens if I leave the page?

Your game is saved automatically, so you can come back later and continue from where you left off.

Can I see my win, loss, and draw record?

Yes, the page shows your record of wins, losses, and draws. This helps you track your progress over time.

Is the game fair?

Yes. The computer only sees the position on the board, just like you. Its strength depends on the level you choose: the lower levels play weaker on purpose, and the highest level is much stronger.

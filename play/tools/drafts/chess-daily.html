How to Approach Today's Chess Puzzle

  • Look for forcing moves first: Check if there are any immediate threats like checks, captures, or pins. These often lead to the correct solution.
  • Think about the opponent's goal: What is the opponent trying to achieve? This can help you predict their next move and find the best counter.
  • Use the hint wisely: If you're stuck, the hint can guide you in the right direction without giving away the full solution. Try to apply what the hint suggests.
  • Visualize the board: Imagine the pieces moving and how the board will look after each possible move. This helps you spot patterns and tactical ideas more easily.
  • Keep your solution clean: Avoid making unnecessary moves. Focus on the most efficient path to solve the puzzle, which often involves fewer steps.

Why This Skill Matters in Real Life

Being able to solve a chess puzzle requires careful analysis, patience, and the ability to think several steps ahead. These are the same skills that help you in exams, interviews, and even everyday decision-making. When you're faced with a complex problem, breaking it down into smaller parts and evaluating each option carefully can lead to the best outcome.

Similarly, in coding interviews or technical assessments, the ability to approach a problem systematically and identify the most efficient solution is highly valued. Practicing chess puzzles helps train your brain to think logically and creatively, which is useful in many areas of life and work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I solve the puzzle on a mobile device?

Yes, the game works on all modern browsers, including those on mobile phones and tablets.

What happens if I make a mistake while solving?

A wrong move is simply taken back, and you can try again or ask for a hint. You still complete the day's puzzle and keep your streak; solve it with no mistakes for a perfect result.

How often does the puzzle change?

A new puzzle appears every day at midnight your local time. Everyone gets the same puzzle on the same day, so you can compare your results with others.

Where do the puzzles come from?

The puzzles are taken from the Lichess puzzle database, which is available under the CC0 license. This means they are free to use and share, and they are based on real games played by chess players around the world.
